CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION (10–15 Pages)
1.1 Background of the Study
Global Context
Education systems around the world generate vast amounts of academic data through learning management systems, assessment records, attendance logs, and digital learning platforms. With the advancement of Data Science and Educational Data Mining, researchers and institutions can analyze these datasets to uncover patterns that influence student learning outcomes.
Educational Data Mining (EDM) involves the application of computational techniques such as machine learning, statistical modeling, and pattern recognition to analyze educational datasets. The goal is to improve teaching strategies, learning environments, and institutional decision-making.
Globally, universities increasingly use predictive analytics to:
Identify students at risk of failure
Improve retention rates
Personalize learning experiences
Support data-driven decision making
Machine learning models such as Decision Tree, Random Forest, and Logistic Regression are widely used to predict academic performance.
African and Kenyan Context
In many African institutions, especially Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) institutions, educational data is mainly used for administrative reporting rather than predictive analysis.
Kenya has expanded its TVET sector to improve technical skills and employability among young people. Institutions such as Kitale National Polytechnic provide training in engineering, ICT, business, and applied sciences.
However, several challenges affect student performance in TVET institutions:
Irregular class attendance
Low continuous assessment scores
Poor study habits
Limited academic monitoring systems
Research shows that early identification of struggling students can significantly improve academic outcomes. Predictive analytics offers an opportunity to transform raw academic data into actionable insights that support students and educators.
1.2 Problem Statement
Despite the availability of academic data in institutions like Kitale National Polytechnic, most student performance evaluations occur only after final examinations.
This approach presents several challenges:
Students struggling academically are identified too late.
Lecturers lack tools to monitor student performance trends.
Institutions cannot implement early interventions.
Although valuable data such as attendance records, continuous assessment test scores, and assignment results are available, they are rarely analyzed using predictive techniques.
Therefore, there is a need to apply educational data mining techniques to develop predictive models that can identify students at risk of poor academic performance early in the academic semester.

Advantages:

Advantages:
